AI Pix Explorer is an interactive textbook game. It teaches how large language models (LLMs) and AI agents work, and it shows where they fail.

The player is a new research fellow at Hollis Hall, a fictional law library. The story lasts one working week, from Monday to Friday. Each chapter has a short scene, a textbook section, a hands-on toy, and a few quiz questions.

## What it covers

The game has 15 chapters, grouped by day. Topics include:

- How text becomes tokens, and how tokens become numbers
- Embeddings and "maps of meaning"
- Attention and how a model chooses the next word
- Sampling, temperature, and why answers vary
- Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) and AI agents
- The known limits of these systems

## Who it is for

The main reader is a lawyer or legal researcher who knows the basics of machine learning and learns best from pictures and hands-on activities. All legal examples are fictional.

## Use it

The game runs in a web browser. It does not need an account, and it does not collect data. Progress saves in the browser.

## How it was made

This project was built with Claude Code, an AI coding assistant. Brianna Imm set the goals, made the design decisions, and reviewed the results. The repository describes the process and its limits.
